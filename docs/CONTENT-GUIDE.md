# Content guide

Great content is what makes a small boutique look big. Two things matter most:
**photography** and **product copy**.

## Product photography — the #1 lever

> The single biggest "professional vs. amateur" difference on the site right now is the
> product photos.

**Avoid**
- Screenshots from Facebook lives with **text/watermarks baked into the image**
  (e.g. "Reet collections", style codes printed on the photo). These read as low-budget.
- Mismatched backgrounds, lighting, and crops across products.

**Aim for**
- A **consistent background** (a plain wall, a clean backdrop, or a tidy outdoor spot).
- A **consistent crop and aspect ratio** — the site uses a tall **4:5** portrait. Shoot
  each item the same way so the grid looks uniform.
- **Good, even lighting** — daylight near a window works well.
- One clear hero shot per item; add detail shots (fabric, embroidery) where you can.
- **No on-image text.** Let the website's design provide the labels and pricing.

**Practical tip:** even re-cropping existing photos to remove watermark bands and matching
the 4:5 ratio noticeably lifts the whole catalog. Cleaning the photos will do more for the
brand than almost any code change.

## Product copy

- **Name:** descriptive and appealing — color + style + piece type
  (e.g. *"Blush Pink Chikankari Anarkali Set"*).
- **Fabric:** name the material (Banarasi silk, georgette, pure cotton…). Customers care.
- **Note/description:** 1–2 warm sentences on how it feels, when to wear it, and what's
  special. Lean into the occasion (wedding guest, festive, everyday).
- **Sizes:** keep the size list accurate; the site has a size guide modal.

## Brand voice

- Warm, personal, and celebratory — like a family boutique, not a faceless store.
- Weave in culture naturally (occasions, festivals, fabric heritage) — see the
  [Brand Guide](./BRAND-GUIDE.md).
- Keep it genuine; avoid hype and generic filler.
